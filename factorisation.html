Factorisation

Factorisation is the process of expressing an algebraic expression as the product of two or more factors. It is one of the most important concepts in algebra and helps simplify expressions, solve equations, and understand polynomial relationships.

Quick Facts

  • ✔ Converts expressions into factors.
  • ✔ Makes solving equations easier.
  • ✔ Used in Algebra and Calculus.
  • ✔ Helps simplify polynomial expressions.
01 Introduction

What is Factorisation?

Factorisation is the process of breaking an algebraic expression into smaller expressions whose product gives the original expression. Each smaller expression is called a factor.

Example

x² + 5x + 6 = (x + 2)(x + 3)

02 Importance

Why is Factorisation Important?

  • Simplifies algebraic expressions.
  • Helps solve quadratic equations.
  • Useful in graphing polynomial functions.
  • Widely used in higher mathematics.
  • Improves problem-solving speed.
03 Method

Common Factor Method

The Common Factor Method is the simplest method of factorisation. In this method, we first find the greatest common factor (GCF) that is common to every term and then take it outside the brackets.

Rule
Find the common factor → Take it outside → Write the remaining terms inside the brackets.

General Formula

Common Factor \[ ab+ac=a(b+c) \]

Examples

Expression Factorised Form
3x + 6 3(x + 2)
5x² + 10x 5x(x + 2)
8a²b + 12ab 4ab(2a + 3)
15xy + 20x 5x(3y + 4)
Solved Example
Factorise: 12x² + 18x

The greatest common factor is 6x.

12x² + 18x

= 6x(2x + 3)
04 Method

Grouping Method

The Grouping Method is used when an algebraic expression has four or more terms. The terms are grouped so that each group has a common factor, making the expression easier to factorise.

Rule
Group the terms → Take out the common factor from each group → Find the common bracket → Write the final factorised form.

General Formula

Grouping \[ ab+ac+db+dc=(a+d)(b+c) \]

Examples

Expression Factorised Form
ax + ay + bx + by (a+b)(x+y)
3x + 3y + 2x + 2y (3+2)(x+y)
x² + 2x + 3x + 6 (x+2)(x+3)
5a + 10b + 2a + 4b (5+2)(a+2b)
Solved Example
Factorise: x² + 5x + 6

x² + 5x + 6
= x² + 2x + 3x + 6
= x(x+2)+3(x+2)

(x+2)(x+3)
04 Formula

Difference of Squares

When two perfect squares are connected by a minus sign, they can be factorised using the Difference of Squares Formula.

Remember
This identity works only when both terms are perfect squares and the sign between them is minus (−).

Main Formula

Identity
a2 − b2 = (a + b)(a − b)

Examples

Expression Factorised Form
x2 − 16 (x + 4)(x − 4)
9a2 − 25 (3a + 5)(3a − 5)
49x2 − y2 (7x + y)(7x − y)
100m2 − 81n2 (10m + 9n)(10m − 9n)
Solved Example
Factorise 64x2 − 25

Since 64 = 82 and 25 = 52 apply the identity.

64x2 − 25

= (8x + 5)(8x − 5)
05 Formulae

Perfect Square Formulae

Perfect Square Formulae are special algebraic identities used to expand and factorise algebraic expressions quickly. These identities are used frequently in Algebra.

Tip
Learn these three identities by heart. They appear in many mathematics problems.

Main Formulae

Identity 1
(a + b)2 = a2 + 2ab + b2
Identity 2
(a − b)2 = a2 − 2ab + b2
Identity 3
a2 − b2 = (a+b)(a−b)

Examples

Expression Result
x2 + 6x + 9 (x + 3)2
a2 − 10a + 25 (a − 5)2
16m2 − 81 (4m+9)(4m−9)
(2x+5)2 4x2+20x+25
Solved Example
Factorise x2 + 14x + 49

49 = 72 and 14x = 2 × 7 × x

x2 + 14x + 49

= (x + 7)2
06 Formulae

Cube Formulae

Cube identities are used to expand and factorise expressions involving cubes. These formulas are very important in algebra and competitive exams.

Remember
These identities are very powerful for fast calculations and simplifications.

Important Identities

Identity 1
(a + b)3 = a3 + 3a2b + 3ab2 + b3
Identity 2
(a − b)3 = a3 − 3a2b + 3ab2 − b3
Identity 3
a3 + b3 = (a + b)(a2 − ab + b2)
Identity 4
a3 − b3 = (a − b)(a2 + ab + b2)

Examples

Expression Factorised Form
x3 + 8 (x + 2)(x2 − 2x + 4)
a3 − 27 (a − 3)(a2 + 3a + 9)
x3 − 64 (x − 4)(x2 + 4x + 16)
(2a − b)3 8a3 − 12a2b + 6ab2 − b3
Solved Example
Factorise x3 − 64

Since 64 = 43, we use: a3 − b3 formula.

x3 − 64

= (x − 4)(x2 + 4x + 16)
07 Method

Quadratic Factorisation

Quadratic expressions are of the form ax² + bx + c. Factorisation helps to split them into two simpler binomial expressions.

Key Idea
Find two numbers whose product is a × c and sum is b.

General Form

Quadratic Form \[ ax^2 + bx + c = (px + q)(rx + s) \]

Examples

Expression Factorised Form
x² + 5x + 6 (x + 2)(x + 3)
x² + 7x + 12 (x + 3)(x + 4)
x² − x − 6 (x − 3)(x + 2)
2x² + 7x + 3 (2x + 1)(x + 3)
Solved Example
Factorise: x² + 9x + 20

We need two numbers whose product = 20 and sum = 9 → (4,5)

(x + 4)(x + 5)
08 Technique

Splitting the Middle Term

This method is used in quadratic expressions of the form ax² + bx + c. We split the middle term into two parts so that grouping becomes possible.

Core Idea
Find two numbers whose product = a × c and sum = b, then split the middle term.

Method Steps

Steps
  1. Multiply a × c
  2. Find two numbers with product and sum
  3. Split middle term
  4. Group terms and factorise

Example

Expression Process Result
2x² + 7x + 3 7x = 6x + x (2x + 1)(x + 3)
x² + 8x + 12 8x = 6x + 2x (x + 2)(x + 6)
Solved Example
Factorise: x² + 11x + 24

24 → (3,8) because 3 + 8 = 11

(x + 3)(x + 8)
