01 Introduction

Basic Concepts

Geometry is the branch of mathematics that studies shapes, sizes, measurements, and the properties of space. Every geometric figure is built from a few fundamental elements:

Point Represents an exact position in space. It has no length, width, or height (no dimensions).
Line Extends infinitely in both directions and has no thickness.
Ray Begins at one endpoint and extends infinitely in one direction.
Line Segment The part of a line bounded by two fixed endpoints.
02 Angles

Types of Angles & Properties

Angle Measure
Acute Angle Between 0° and 90°
Right Angle Exactly 90°
Obtuse Angle Between 90° and 180°
Straight Angle Exactly 180°
Reflex Angle Between 180° and 360°
Complementary Angles Two angles whose sum is 90°.
Supplementary Angles Two angles whose sum is 180°.

When a transversal intersects two parallel lines, the corresponding angles are equal and the alternate angles are also equal.

03 Triangles

Triangles — Types & Properties

Based on Sides Properties
Equilateral Triangle All three sides are equal, each angle is 60°
Isosceles Triangle Two sides are equal
Scalene Triangle All three sides are unequal
Sum of Interior Angles \[ \angle A + \angle B + \angle C = 180^\circ \]
Exterior Angle Theorem Exterior Angle = Sum of the two remote interior angles.
Triangle Inequality: The sum of any two sides of a triangle must always be greater than the third side; otherwise, a triangle cannot be formed.
04 Theorem

Pythagorean Theorem

In a right-angled triangle, the square of the hypotenuse is equal to the sum of the squares of the other two sides.

Pythagorean Theorem \[ c^2 = a^2 + b^2 \]

Common Pythagorean Triples (integer side lengths):

Triple a, b, c
1 3, 4, 5
2 5, 12, 13
3 8, 15, 17
4 7, 24, 25
05 Comparison

Congruence & Similarity

Congruence: Two triangles are congruent if they have exactly the same shape and size. Common tests: SSS, SAS, ASA, AAS, RHS.

Similarity: Two triangles are similar if their corresponding angles are equal and their corresponding sides are proportional. Common tests: AA, SSS, SAS.

Area Ratio of Similar Triangles \[ \frac{\text{Area}_1}{\text{Area}_2} = \left( \frac{\text{Side}_1}{\text{Side}_2} \right)^2 \]
06 Quadrilaterals

Types & Properties of Quadrilaterals

A quadrilateral is a polygon with four sides, four vertices, and four angles. The sum of all interior angles of every quadrilateral is 360°. Different quadrilaterals have different properties based on their sides, angles, and diagonals.

Shape Sides Angles Diagonals
Square All four sides are equal All angles are 90° Equal, perpendicular, bisect each other and the angles
Rectangle Opposite sides are equal and parallel All angles are 90° Equal and bisect each other
Parallelogram Opposite sides are equal and parallel Opposite angles are equal Bisect each other
Rhombus All four sides are equal Opposite angles are equal Perpendicular, bisect each other, and bisect angles
Trapezium One pair of opposite sides is parallel No fixed property No fixed property
Isosceles Trapezium One pair of parallel sides; non-parallel sides are equal Base angles are equal Equal diagonals
Kite (Deltoid) Two pairs of adjacent equal sides One pair of opposite angles are equal Perpendicular; one diagonal bisects the other
Cyclic Quadrilateral All four vertices lie on a circle Opposite angles are supplementary (sum = 180°) No fixed property
Tangential Quadrilateral Has an inscribed circle touching all four sides No fixed property No fixed property
Irregular Quadrilateral No special side relationship No special angle relationship No fixed property
Interior Angle Sum \[ \angle A+\angle B+\angle C+\angle D=360^\circ \]
Square Area \[ \text{Area}=a^2 \]
Rectangle Area \[ \text{Area}=l\times b \]
Parallelogram Area \[ \text{Area}=b\times h \]
Rhombus Area \[ \text{Area}=\frac{d_1\times d_2}{2} \]
Trapezium Area \[ \text{Area}=\frac{1}{2}(a+b)\times h \]
Important: Every square is a rectangle and a rhombus, but not every rectangle or rhombus is a square. Likewise, every rectangle and rhombus is a parallelogram, but every parallelogram is not a rectangle or a rhombus.
07 Circle

Circle (Geometry)

A circle is the set of all points in a plane that are at the same distance from a fixed point called the center. This fixed distance is called the radius.

Radius & Diameter \[ d = 2r \]
Circumference \[ C = 2\pi r = \pi d \]
Area of Circle \[ A = \pi r^2 \]
Arc Length \[ L=\frac{\theta}{360^\circ}\times2\pi r \]
Area of Sector \[ A=\frac{\theta}{360^\circ}\times\pi r^2 \]
Tangent Property A tangent is always perpendicular to the radius at the point of contact.
Part of Circle Description
Center The fixed point of the circle.
Radius Line segment from the center to any point on the circle.
Diameter A chord passing through the center (largest chord).
Chord A line segment joining any two points on the circle.
Arc A part of the circumference.
Sector Region enclosed by two radii and an arc.
Segment Region enclosed by a chord and an arc.
Tangent A line touching the circle at exactly one point.
Secant A line intersecting the circle at two points.
Important: The diameter is the longest chord of a circle, and a tangent is always perpendicular to the radius at the point of contact.

13 Examples

Solved Examples

Example 1
A right triangle has sides 6 cm and 8 cm. Find the hypotenuse.

Solution: \(c^2=6^2+8^2=36+64=100\)

c = 10 cm
Example 2
Find the area of a triangle whose sides are 5 cm, 6 cm and 7 cm.

Solution: \(s=\dfrac{5+6+7}{2}=9\)
Area \(=\sqrt{9\times4\times3\times2}=\sqrt{216}\)

Area ≈ 14.7 cm²
Example 3
Find the area and circumference of a circle with radius 7 cm. (π = 22/7)

Solution: Area \(=\tfrac{22}{7}\times7^2=154\), Circumference \(=2\times\tfrac{22}{7}\times7=44\)

Area = 154 cm², Circumference = 44 cm
Example 4
Find the volume of a cylinder with radius 7 cm and height 10 cm.

Solution: \(V=\pi r^2h=\tfrac{22}{7}\times49\times10\)

V = 1540 cm³
14 Practice

Practice Yourself

Tap on a question to reveal the answer.

1. A rectangle has a length of 12 cm and a width of 5 cm. Find its diagonal.
Diagonal \(=\sqrt{12^2+5^2}=\sqrt{169}\). Diagonal = 13 cm
2. An equilateral triangle has a side length of 8 cm. Find its perimeter.
Perimeter = 24 cm
3. Find the volume of a sphere with radius 14 cm. (π = 22/7)
\(V=\tfrac43\times\tfrac{22}{7}\times14^3\). V ≈ 11498.7 cm³
4. The angles of a triangle are in the ratio 3:4:5. Find the largest angle.
Total parts = 12, each part = 15°. Largest angle = 5 × 15°. 75°
5. Find the distance between the points (2,3) and (6,6).
\(d=\sqrt{(6-2)^2+(6-3)^2}=\sqrt{16+9}=\sqrt{25}\). d = 5 units
